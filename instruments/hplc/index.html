Pharmacopoeial assays enforce strict system suitability criteria. Deformed peaks indicate hardware failure, chemical overloading, or secondary interactions.

🌳 Method Development Decision Tree & Linear Solvent Strength (LSS) Model

Under the Linear Solvent Strength (LSS) model, analyte retention factor ($k'$) drops exponentially as organic modifier volume fraction ($\phi$) increases: $\log k' = \log k_w - S\phi$.

Step 1: Analyte Ionization Assessment

• Acidic Drug (e.g. Aspirin, Ibuprofen, pKa ~3.5–4.4): Lower buffer pH to 2.5 (suppresses ionization, retains strongly on C18).
• Basic Drug (e.g. Quinine, Metronidazole): Use pH 2.0 or pH 7.0 with ion-pairing modifier to prevent silanol tailing.

Step 2: Isocratic vs Gradient Elution

• Δk' < 20: Use Isocratic Elution (e.g. 50:50 ACN/Water) for simple tablets. Zero column re-equilibration time.
• Δk' > 20: Use Organic Gradient Ramp (e.g. 10% → 90% B over 10 min) to elute hydrophobic impurities in a single run.
