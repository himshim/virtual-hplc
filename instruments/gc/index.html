🌡️ Oven Temperature Programming & The General Elution Problem

Solve early-peak congestion and late-peak broadening in complex volatile mixtures:

Linear Temperature Program (50°C → 250°C)

As the oven temperature rises linearly, vapor pressures of high-boiling analytes increase exponentially, accelerating their migration down the capillary column.

  • Uniform Peak Widths: Unlike isothermal runs, all peaks maintain narrow, sharp gaussian shapes throughout the chromatogram.
  • Short Analysis Time: High-boilers (e.g. C20:4 FAMEs, Geraniol) elute in minutes rather than hours.
  • Improved Detection Limits: Taller, narrower peaks dramatically improve signal-to-noise ratio ($S/N$).

🪜 Kovats Retention Index (I) Derivation & n-Alkane Scale

The Kovats Retention Index ($I$) normalizes retention times relative to homologous $n$-alkanes ($C_z$, where $I = 100 \times z$), making chromatographic data universally reproducible across different column lengths, carrier gas velocities, and lab models: I = 100 · [z + (log t'Rx - log t'Rz) / (log t'R(z+1) - log t'Rz)]

🔥 FID Effective Carbon Number (ECN) & Response Factor Calculator

The Flame Ionization Detector (FID) produces current proportional to oxidizable carbon atoms forming $\text{CHO}^+$ ions in a Hydrogen/Air flame. Non-hydrocarbon functional groups reduce the effective carbon count per Sternberg's empirical rules:

Effective Carbon (ECN)

Primary alcohol carbon bonded to -OH has reduced ionization efficiency ($2.0 - 0.5 = 1.50\text{ ECN}$).
