🔴 Bathochromic (Red Shift)
Shift to longer λ (lower energy). Example: Phenol Red (433→558 nm) & Paracetamol in alkali (243→257 nm).
🔵 Hypsochromic (Blue Shift)
Shift to shorter λ (higher energy). Example: Protonation of anilines removing nitrogen lone pair conjugation.
⬆ Hyperchromic Shift
Increase in molar absorptivity ε. Example: DNA thermal denaturation (unstacking bases increases A260 by ~35%).
⬇ Hypochromic Shift
Decrease in molar absorptivity ε. Example: dsDNA base-stacking in native double helix reduces A260.

📉 Stray Light & High-Absorbance Non-Linearity Sandbox

The Beer-Lambert Law ($A = \varepsilon \cdot c \cdot l$) is strictly linear only for monochromatic light. Real instruments experience stray light fraction ($s$), causing apparent absorbance to flatten at $A > 2.0\text{ AU}$: A_apparent = -log₁₀(10^(-A_true) + s)

True Absorbance (A_true): 2.00 AU

💡 Optimal linear assay range is 0.2 – 1.5 AU. Above 2.0 AU, dilute your sample to restore Beer-Lambert linearity.

📊 Derivative Spectroscopy Masterclass (dA/dλ & d²A/dλ²)

Resolve heavily overlapping multicomponent drug peaks (e.g. Paracetamol + Caffeine) without physical HPLC separation:

Zero-Order Absorption Spectrum A(λ)

Standard absorbance spectrum showing broad, overlapping Gaussian bands. Minor components appear only as subtle shoulders on larger peaks.

  • Zero-Crossing Points: In 1st derivative ($dA/d\lambda = 0$), exact inflection and peak apex positions are pinpointed.
  • Negative Minima in 2nd Deriv: Absorption peaks invert into sharp negative troughs with dramatically enhanced resolution ($W_{1/2} \approx 0.53 \times W$).
  • Turbidity Elimination: Constant baseline offsets ($\frac{d(C)}{d\lambda} = 0$) drop out completely.
